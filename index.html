Three steps, all year

DeductBook is the folder you wish you'd kept. Add gifts as they happen, attach the paper, and the summary writes itself in March.

1

Record the donation

Money, goods, stock, mileage or an out-of-pocket volunteer cost. The form asks only for what the IRS asks for, and a value guide prices donated goods at thrift-store fair market value.

2

Attach the receipt

Snap the receipt or the charity's thank-you letter. DeductBook tells you when a gift of $250 or more still needs a written acknowledgment, and when goods over $500 need Form 8283.

3

Hand over the summary

Totals by type, donor and organization, a filing checklist, and a CSV your preparer can open. Every entry shows whether its records are complete.

What's inside

Built for the whole picture of a household's giving, not just the donation pile.

Every kind of gift

Cash, check and card. Goods with itemized values. Stock with cost basis and holding period. Volunteer mileage at the statutory 14¢, plus parking, supplies and travel, with the rules for what counts applied as you type.

Documentation, checked

Each donation shows Records OK, Acknowledgment needed, or Not eligible, with the reason in plain English. Recorded value and deductible value are kept distinct.

One household ledger

Invite your spouse; both of you add donations and receipts to the same records from any device. Changes appear live, and simultaneous edits are kept, never silently overwritten.

Receipts that travel

Photos and PDFs stay attached to their donations, stored privately for your household, and included in every backup.

A value guide with sources

Typical thrift-store ranges for about 130 common items, adjusted by condition, with the IRS rules on appraisals and similar-item aggregation built in.

Accountant-ready export

A tax summary, an entry-level CSV, and an itemized goods CSV that maps to Form 8283. Read-only access for a preparer, if you want to give it.

Why we built it

For years our family tracked charitable gifts in ItsDeductible. When it was discontinued, we went looking for a replacement and didn't find one that covered everything: the cash gifts, the volunteer miles, the receipts and acknowledgment letters that make a deduction hold up, and a way for two people to keep one set of records.

So we built the replacement we wanted, and took the time to make it better than what we'd lost.

Questions

Is this tax advice?

No. DeductBook organizes your records and explains the IRS rules in plain English, citing Publication 526 and 561. Confirm deductions with a qualified preparer. It doesn't file returns and doesn't promise a refund.

Where is my data stored?

Without an account, only in your browser; nothing is uploaded. With a household account, in a database and private file storage where access is limited to the people in your household. Either way you can export everything.

How does household sharing work?

Donations belong to a household, not a person. The owner invites people by email. Members can add and edit; viewers, like an accountant, can only read and export. If two people edit the same donation at once, both versions are kept for you to choose from.

What about volunteer expenses?

Mileage is deductible at 14¢ a mile plus parking and tolls. Supplies, uniforms with no everyday use, and travel away from home on real charity duty count. Your time, local meals, and childcare don't. DeductBook applies these rules as you fill in the form.

Can I import from ItsDeductible?

Not directly yet. You can start with this year's gifts and value items with the same kind of guide. A spreadsheet importer is on the roadmap.
